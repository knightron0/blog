These are a few problems I care about and hope to contribute to. I don’t have neat answers to them; this is a running list of ideas I’m trying to understand (hopefully these get refined with time). If any of these sound interesting to you, **please** [reach out](https://x.com/msarthak29)!

#### Systems
- Since high-performance code is becoming cheap now, what does the future systems stack look like? Can we [retire the intermediate abstractions](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2026-08-05-retire-the-abstractions), keep a high-level [spec](https://docs.pytorch.org/devlogs/compiler/2026-07-25-pytorch-a-reference-language/) as the source of truth, and generate optimized code? Do we need to [formally](https://gimletlabs.ai/blog/formally-verifying-ai-generated-kernels) [verify](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YquydxO9Gzk) [against](https://srush.github.io/lean-transformer/#System-Optimization) [the spec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22631)?
- How can we improve [intelligence per dollar (or watt)](https://www.intelligence-per-watt.ai/)? We seem to be making progress on the frontier by biting the bullet on [ridiculous orders of scaling](https://willdepue.substack.com/p/a-stargate-for-data); it is time for efficiency to follow. Does this come from [better](https://pytorch.org/blog/paretoq-scaling-laws-in-extremely-low-bit-llm-quantization/) [algorithms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08606), [better hardware-architecture](https://www.maartengrootendorst.com/blog/mamba/#hardware-aware-algorithm) [co-design](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/flash4/), or [cheaper compute](https://geohot.github.io//blog/jekyll/update/2023/05/24/the-tiny-corp-raised-5M.html)?
- I agree [with this](https://x.com/srush_nlp/status/2115199243304718712?a=m54fy6A3p86frBagtpM&s=20). As proof synthesis gets easier, is [specification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13077) becoming the real bottlenecks to ubiquitous formal verification? Or do we need to rethink our programming abstractions entirely?
- Do we need more bitter-lesson pilled compilers? [tinygrad](https://github.com/tinygrad/tinygrad) is a step towards that, but not quite there yet. From my limited knowledge of compilers -- would we want to find better (maybe learned?) search algorithms? or expand the representative power itself? 

#### Modelling
- There has been [a lot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37899) [of recent work](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bfVJ4GsHrO) on [zeroth-order optimization](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust), but comparatively little work on inherently non-differentiable architectures. The real promise (imo) lies in enabling fundamentally new architectures where differentiable relaxations come at a **significant** cost to performance or expressivity, can we find these? 
- Co-designing the entire stack (architecture, optimizer, training recipe) will become [increasingly feasible](https://www.coreauto.com/blog/when-ai-starts-writing-systems-code) as high-performance systems become a function of search and compute. Are we leaving performance on the table by optimizing these components independently? 
- How would [RSI](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement) work if future progress requires a fundamental paradigm shift? If both us/models are stuck in local optimums, would we need new core learning theories? Ones that are OOMs more sample efficient?
- This is on a lot of minds at the moment, but how can we avoid [these](https://x.com/sama/status/2099352016988614852) [scenarios](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)? How is alignment best tackled: scientifically, institutionally, or through government?

#### Abstractions & Interfaces
- What does the next generation of interfaces look like? How can we make [malleable software](https://www.inkandswitch.com/malleable-software/) ubiquitous? Are current efforts ([one](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/) and [two](https://runway.com/news/research/introducing-solaris)) steps in the right direction? What if we augment all deterministic workflows with intelligence?
- Moving from assembly to high-level languages required us to **trust** compilers without inspecting the generated machine code. As natural language is now a programming abstraction, what would it take to similarly fully trust intelligence to write software? Do we need to [rethink how we specify intent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17150), [verify correctness](https://georgwiese.github.io/posts/formal-verification-ai/), and reason about code we no longer write or even read? 
- The definition of "work" [has changed so much in the past century](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/scott-wu-8b94ab96_picture-one-of-your-ancestors-from-hundreds-share-7488289761728831489-u9IH/). In a post-AGI world, [how will this evolve](https://charlesponeill.com/posts/final-form-of-work/)? 

#### Future?
I believe AI will enable humanity to understand orders of magnitude more about the world, the universe, and reality than we can even _imagine_ today. One day, I want to work on all of the "impossible" moonshot problems that piqued my interest as a kid:
- How much of human civilization has been lost to time? Can we recover forgotten languages, reconstruct ancient cultures (see [Vesuvius Challenge](https://scrollprize.org/)), and preserve the collective knowledge of previous generations? I think immortalizing humanity's accumulated knowledge is incredibly meaningful!
- There are potentially trillions of galaxies in the observable universe, and we have barely scratched the surface of what's out there. Are we alone? We've already discovered [over 6,000 exoplanets](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/) in just a few decades. Imagine what we could discover with dramatically more capable scientific tools!
- We are yet to catalog all the species sharing our **own** planet. In 2025, we found [72 new species](https://blog.calacademy.org/press/releases/california-academy-of-sciences-describes-72-new-species-in-2025). Could we eventually discover and understand every form of life on Earth? In a similar vein, we have visually observed [less than 0.001%](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adp8602) of the deep-ocean seafloor. What entirely unknown phenomena or ecosystems are still hiding beneath the surface?
